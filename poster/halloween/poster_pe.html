Perception Encoder: The best visual embeddings
are not at the output of the network

One contrastive recipe, every task — if you align with the right layers

Central claim — After a scaled image pretraining recipe + robust video data engine, contrastive vision-language training alone yields strong, general embeddings for classification, captioning, and localization. The catch: they hide in the intermediate layers, so two alignment methods draw them out.

01 · PROBLEM One encoder per task is a tax

Traditionally each downstream family wanted its own pretraining objective — classification, captioning, localization each pulled the encoder's features to a different "best" layer, so a single output embedding could not serve all.

PE's reframing: train one contrastive vision-language recipe at scale, and the general-purpose embedding exists — just not where the output head looks.

04 · READING NOTES Simplification story

A zoo of tailored pretraining objectives collapses into one contrastive recipe + two alignment heads. Released: models, code, and PVD — a dataset of synthetically and human-annotated videos. The video data engine recaptioned video samples from 0M (image-only baseline) up to 17M samples during scaling (Fig. 6).

06 · HOW TO READ Three benchmark families, three protocols

86.6 / 76.9 — zero-shot classification & retrieval (image robustness avg on ImageNet variants, video on Kinetics-400) · 94.6 / 80.9 / 82.7 — document/image/video Q&A through an MLLM pipeline with an 8B LLM · 66.0 — system-level COCO detection against leading COCO results (Table 15). Not one leaderboard — three.

07 · FAMILY S/B/L — one recipe, scaled

The family spans model steps S/14 · B/14 · L/14; Fig. 2 tunes the pretraining recipe on a fixed data sweep, and Figs. 3–4 show the recipe's gains persist across model sizes (S/B/L) and training steps (120K → 360K).

Why contrastive alone is enough here: the recipe + video engine create the alignment conditions; the two alignment heads then route the same features to language or spatial heads. One backbone, no per-task pretraining detours.

09 · INSIGHT Where the good embedding hides

The loaded insight for anyone reusing vision encoders: don't tap the last layer by default. Fig. 8 evaluates intermediate layers as frozen features across tasks for different pretraining methods — the sweet layer shifts by objective, and PE's contrastive recipe moves it predictably; alignment heads do the routing.

Why "language gap" matters: the language alignment exists precisely because raw mid-layer embeddings are not language-ready; the alignment is the bridge that keeps both dialogue and spatial heads served.

10 · OPEN LIMITS What remains unresolved?

Latency/FLOP trade-offs per family member (Table 4 configurations list sizes; cost curves are in the paper's appendix scales), and the optimal alignment layer per model size is an empirical map (Figs. 8·10–12), not a closed formula. Best-at-scale claims are bounded to the evaluated task set.

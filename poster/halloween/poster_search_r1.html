01 · PROBLEM Why prompted RAG isn't enough

  • External, up-to-date knowledge is essential for reasoning — but a model prompted to search may lack the capability of interacting optimally with the engine.
  • Standard RAG pipelines insert retrieval at fixed points, decided by an external schedule — not by the reasoner.

Search-R1 makes the retrieval policy learned behavior: when to search, what to query, how to reformulate across turns.

At 7B the PPO variant leads GRPO by +0.081 avg (0.431 vs 0.350) — yet Fig. 2a shows GRPO converging faster with late-training instability. Optimizer choice interacts with scale and init; the paper's headline run is the steadier PPO.

04 · KEY DESIGN Two stabilizers

Retrieved token masking. Retrieved text is environment output, not the policy's own action — masking it out of the RL credit assignment keeps training stable (policy is credited on what it wrote). Table 4: training with retrieved-token loss masking achieves consistently higher performance across the tested LLMs.

Simple outcome reward. Reward on the final answer, not on search behavior — so search emerges as a means, not a target.

Fig. 2 insights: GRPO generally converges faster but may exhibit instability after a number of training steps, while PPO is slower but steadier; base vs instruct and response-length dynamics further shape the recipe.

05 · READING NOTES From RAG to learned retrieval

Fixed-point RAG treats retrieval as glue around the reasoner. Search-R1 folds retrieval into the reasoner's decision space — a cousin of tool-use RL, but trained on end-to-end QA outcomes only.

09 · OPEN LIMITS What remains unresolved?

The search engine and corpus are fixed in the experiment — performance claims are engine-bound, not universal. Response-length dynamics (Fig. 2c) and multi-turn count effects leave cost/quality trade-offs to explore. GRPO's late-training instability (Fig. 2a) is an open optimizer question.

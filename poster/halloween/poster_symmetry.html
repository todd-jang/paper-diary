Left-Right Symmetry Breaking in CLIP-style VLMs

A controllable 1D testbed for how relational (left/right) competence actually forms — and a mechanistic answer

Central claim — CLIP-style contrastive training on a 1D image-text testbed does learn left-right relations, and an attention decomposition shows the mechanism: positional × token embedding interactions induce a horizontal attention gradient that breaks left-right symmetry. Ablate it, and discrimination collapses.

01 · PROBLEM Does a VLM really know "left of"?

Spatial understanding remains a key challenge in vision-language models — and it is unclear whether apparent competence is truly acquired, and through what mechanism. Known symptoms in CLIP-style models include weak parity between mirrored relations.

Instead of benchmarking a giant model, this paper builds the smallest controllable setting in which relational competence demonstrably forms — so the emergence can be studied, not just scored.

Lightweight Transformer-based vision and text encoders are trained end-to-end on one- and two-object scenes, then evaluated on generalization to unseen object pairs while varying label diversity and layout diversity systematically.

03 · HOW TO READ A toy testbed with a scope

Everything is deliberately minimal: 1D scenes with one or two objects, lightweight Transformer encoders, CLIP-style contrastive loss. "Generalization" means unseen object pairs inside this testbed — that controlled scale is what makes mechanism claims possible at all.

04 · FINDING A Contrastive training learns left-right — conditionally

  • Contrastive training does acquire left-right relational competence in this testbed.
  • Label diversity matters more than layout diversity — the primary driver of generalization to unseen pairs.
  • So the "does it generalize?" answer depends on how varied the descriptions are, not merely how the scene is arranged.
Data design implication: to teach relational grounding, invest in describing the same relation in many ways — mirroring augmentation alone is not the lever.

05 · READING NOTES Three levels of generalization

The testbed scores three distinct generalization types (Figs. 3·9·11): single-object positional (does an object's position map to "left"/"right" text), seen-pair (relations for object pairs observed in training), and unseen-pair (compositional transfer to novel pairs). Training dynamics (Fig. 11) track when each level switches on.

Why "symmetry breaking" is the right frame: mirror-symmetric inputs yield symmetric representations unless something breaks the symmetry — here the break is shown to be created by the model's own positional interaction, a mechanistic complement to dataset-level bias accounts.

06 · FINDING B Symmetry is broken inside attention

Decomposing the pre-softmax attention logit (Figs. 5·12–14) isolates which term carries the left/right signal: interactions between positional and token embeddings induce a horizontal attention gradient — a stable left/right asymmetry in how heads attend along the scene axis. The reduced 1-layer model (Fig. 4) isolates the effect with a minimal parameter budget.

07 · WHAT TUNES IT Levers on the breaking

  • Weight decay — Fig. 10 scans it and shows unseen-pair generalization depends on the value: the regularization level shapes whether the symmetry-breaking solution survives.
  • Label vs layout diversity — systematic variation shows label diversity is the primary driver (Figs. 3·8).
  • Embedding geometry — Fig. 7 probes image/text token alignment via learned rotation matrices, connecting the gradient to representational space.
Mechanism check: the strongest evidence pairing (Fig. 14 present & generalizing vs Fig. 15 absent & non-generalizing) shows the gradient tracks competence — correlation aligned with the ablation's causal cut.

08 · CONFIG & PROTOCOL How the toy is built — Figs 4·10

  • Fig 4 — reduced 1-layer model: MB = Mrep = 1, Mh = 4 — the minimal setting where the symmetry either breaks on its own or does not.
  • Fig 10 — generalizing config: MB = 2, Mrep = 2, Mh = 4, dhead = 32 — the parameterization behind the mechanism scans.
  • Figs 12·13 — weight/bias decomposition of the breaking pathway; Fig 15 is the non-generalizing control that lacks it.

09 · OPEN LIMITS What remains unresolved?

Transfer of the mechanism to full-resolution, multi-object scenes and to production VLMs is beyond the testbed's evidence; the paper's own discussion frames the boundary. Production-scale validation would repeat the decomposition pipeline on trained CLIP checkpoints.
